About the Project

During my time in the Human-Centered Design and Development capstone course, my team developed Course Clarity, an AI-powered chatbot designed to assist students by answering questions about course syllabi. This project aimed to streamline access to important course information, reducing confusion and helping students make informed academic decisions.

Building Course Clarity required integrating a large dataset of syllabi into a structured database, designing a natural language processing (NLP) model to interpret student queries, and developing a user-friendly interface for seamless interaction. I leveraged my skills in Python to process and manage course data while ensuring accurate and context-aware responses.

This experience deepened my understanding of AI-driven solutions, database management, and user-centered design. It also reinforced my ability to translate complex technical concepts into practical tools that improve student experiences.



Problem Statement

Problem

College students struggle to find reliable, accurate information about courses and professors. Students rely on the website Rate My Professors (RMP) and other unreliable resources when planning their courses, However, Reviews on RMP often contain bias and rely on subjective criteria. Existing content is anonymous and not verified, influencing important academic decisions.

Solution

An AI chatbot application trained on course syllabi that allows students to ask questions and get accurate, up-to-date information that helps that make more informed decisions for academic planning.




Research

Competitor Analysis

Along with my group, I identified the course selection process as one that could be improved upon. Relating to similar experiences of scrolling through endless reviews on RateMyProfessors, we wondered if there was a way to leverage this data more effectively. Further research, including a study conducted by the University of Texas title "Rate My Professors: A Study Of Bias and Inaccuracies In Anonymous Self-Reporting", revealed the problem was even bigger than we originally thought.

The study compared data from RateMyProfessors to ground truth, which was gathered using legitimate, in-person reviews conducted anonymously by students. Ratings were then compared for both male and female professors, as well as STEM or "quantitative" courses versus non-STEM or "qualitative" courses. The results showed a strong negative corrrelation in negative reviews and STEM courses, particularly for female professors. Additionally, the lack of vetting for accuracy is obvious from reviews clearly violating the terms of service while marked as "reviewed".

⚠️Pain Points

  • Bias and inaccuracy as a result of unreliable data
  • Information overload from too many reviews
  • Difficulty extracting critical information from reviews.
